“Audit” means four different things in India, and a new founder usually needs only one or two of them. This guide separates them and lays out the annual cycle so nothing arrives as a surprise penalty.

## The four audits

| Audit | Law | Who | Trigger | Done by | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Statutory audit** | Companies Act | Every company (OPC, private, Section 8) | Always, from year one, even at zero revenue | Chartered Accountant appointed by the company | Audited financial statements + auditor’s report filed in AOC-4 |
| **LLP audit** | LLP Act | LLPs | Turnover > ₹40 lakh **or** contribution > ₹25 lakh | CA | Audited accounts with Form 8 |
| **Tax audit** | Income-tax Act 2025, s.63 | Any business/profession | Turnover > ₹1 crore (₹10 crore if cash receipts and payments each ≤ 5%); professionals > ₹50 lakh | CA | Form 26 (old 3CA/3CB-3CD) by 30 September |
| **GST reconciliation** | CGST Act | Registered persons | Turnover > ₹5 crore | Self-certified (since FY 2020-21) | GSTR-9C with GSTR-9 by 31 December |

Internal audit, cost audit and secretarial audit apply only to much larger companies (e.g., secretarial audit for listed and large public companies; internal audit for private companies above ₹200 crore turnover or ₹100 crore borrowings). CARO (the detailed auditor’s checklist) does **not** apply to small companies and OPCs.

Proprietors and partnership firms have no statutory audit — only the tax audit if they cross the limits, and none at all under presumptive taxation.

## Small company — the relaxations you get

Since **1 December 2025** a private company with paid-up capital **≤ ₹10 crore** and turnover **≤ ₹100 crore** is a small company (holding/subsidiary companies and Section 8 companies are excluded). It gets: annual return in **MGT-7A** signed by a director (no CS needed), only **two board meetings** a year at least 90 days apart, no cash-flow statement, no mandatory auditor rotation, no CARO, abridged directors’ report and halved penalties for many defaults. Most startups qualify until they raise a large round.

## The company year, in order

| What | Deadline | Form | Late consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board meetings | 2/year (small) or 4/year | Minutes | Penalty ₹25,000 company + ₹5,000 officer |
| Deposits / loans return | 30 June | DPT-3 | ₹5,000 + ₹500/day |
| Director KYC | 30 September | DIR-3 KYC / web KYC | DIN deactivated; ₹5,000 to reactivate |
| Annual General Meeting | 30 September (first AGM: within 9 months of the first FY end) | — | Penalty up to ₹1 lakh + ₹5,000/day |
| Auditor appointment/re-appointment | 15 days after AGM | ADT-1 | ₹100/day |
| Financial statements | 30 days after AGM | AOC-4 (XBRL for larger companies) | ₹100/day, no cap |
| Annual return | 60 days after AGM | MGT-7 / MGT-7A | ₹100/day, no cap |
| MSME dues outstanding > 45 days | 30 April and 31 October | MSME-1 | Penalty |
| Beneficial owners above 10% (if any) | 30 days of change | BEN-2 | Penalty |
| Income-tax return | 31 October | ITR-6 | ₹5,000 + interest; losses lost |
| Tax audit report | 30 September | Form 26 | 0.5% of turnover, max ₹1.5 lakh |

OPCs skip the AGM; their AOC-4 is due within 180 days of the year end.

## LLP year

Form 11 by **30 May**, Form 8 by **30 October**, DIR-3 KYC by 30 September, ITR-5 by 31 July (31 October if audited). No AGM, no board minutes mandated. Late fee ₹100/day per form, now tiered for small LLPs but still uncapped in effect.

## Appointing and working with an auditor

- The board appoints the **first auditor within 30 days** of incorporation (else members within 90 days); the first AGM appoints an auditor for five years.
- The auditor cannot be the same person who keeps your books — keep bookkeeping (any accountant) and audit (independent CA firm) separate.
- Fees for a small company: ₹10,000–50,000 for the statutory audit; tax audit adds ₹10,000–30,000.
- They will ask for: trial balance and ledgers from software with **audit trail** enabled, bank statements and reconciliations, sales and purchase registers tied to GST returns, fixed-asset register, loan agreements and confirmations, payroll and TDS registers, statutory registers and minutes, shareholding and share certificates, related-party transactions, inventory records, and management representation letters.
- Expect the report to flag going-concern doubts for loss-making startups — standard, but investors read it.

## Tax audit notes

- Turnover for the ₹1 crore / ₹10 crore test is measured per **PAN** across all businesses; GST is excluded if separately shown.
- Presumptive taxpayers who declare *less* than the presumptive rate and exceed the basic exemption must get audited.
- Form 26 (3CD) reports TDS compliance, MSME payment delays, cash transactions, loans accepted/repaid in cash, and GST turnover reconciliation — your bookkeeping during the year decides how painful this is.

## Penalty arithmetic worth remembering

ROC late fees are **₹100 per day per form with no upper limit**. A company that forgets AOC-4 and MGT-7 for one year pays roughly ₹73,000 in additional fees alone; directors of companies that miss three consecutive years are disqualified for five years. Set the calendar the day you incorporate.
