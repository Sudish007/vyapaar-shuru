“Startup” in Indian law is a status, not a stage. The Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) **recognises** entities that meet a definition, and recognition is the gateway to a set of benefits — some valuable, some symbolic. Over 2.07 lakh entities were recognised by end-2025; about 55,000 were added in FY 2025-26 alone.

## Eligibility (as published on Startup India, October 2026)

- **Entity**: private limited company, LLP, registered partnership firm or cooperative society. Proprietorships and OPC-free solo founders cannot apply as individuals (an OPC qualifies as a private company).
- **Age**: up to **10 years** from incorporation (**20 years** for deep-tech startups).
- **Turnover**: under **₹200 crore** in every financial year (**₹300 crore** for deep-tech).
- **Work**: innovation, development or improvement of products, processes or services, or a scalable model with potential for employment or wealth creation.
- **Not** formed by splitting or reconstructing an existing business; **not** incorporated outside India.

## How to apply

1. Create a **National Single Window System** (nsws.gov.in) account and add the form “Registration as a Startup” (the legacy startupindia.gov.in form redirects here).
2. Upload the certificate of incorporation/registration, PAN, a short note on what is innovative or scalable, and proof of concept — website, pitch deck, video, patents, letters from customers.
3. Expect a certificate in **1–3 weeks**; queries come by email. The whole thing is **free** — DPIIT has appointed no agents and charges no fee.

## What you get

### Tax
- Apply for the **tax holiday** (old section 80-IAC): 100% deduction of profits for any **three consecutive years out of the first ten** — only private limited companies and LLPs, incorporated after 1 April 2016 and before 1 April 2030, after a separate Inter-Ministerial Board approval (Form 1 on the portal with financials and pitch). Approval rates improved after the 2025 revamp (hundreds approved per meeting), but it is still a selection, and MAT/AMT may still apply.
- **Angel tax** (section 56(2)(viib)) no longer exists for any company from 1 April 2025, so the old DPIIT exemption form is moot; notices for FY 2024-25 and earlier can still be defended with the recognition.
- **ESOP tax deferral** for employees of eligible startups (TDS on perquisite deferred up to 48 months from the end of the exercise year, or sale, or leaving — whichever is first).
- Carry forward of losses despite shareholding changes, if all original shareholders continue (section 79 relaxation for eligible startups).

### Compliance
- **Self-certification** under six labour laws and three environment laws (White-category industries) for five years, with inspections only on credible complaint.
- Faster **winding up** (90 days) under the IBC fast-track process.

### IP
- **80% rebate on patent fees**, **50% rebate on trademark fees**, fast-tracked patent examination, free facilitators (SIPP scheme) whose fees the government pays.

### Money and market
- **Startup India Seed Fund Scheme**: grants up to ₹20 lakh for proof of concept/prototype and up to ₹50 lakh as convertible debentures/debt for market entry, through DPIIT-empanelled incubators.
- **Fund of Funds for Startups** (SIDBI-managed; FoF 2.0 approved in 2026) invests in SEBI-registered VC funds, which then invest in startups — indirect, but it keeps capital in the system.
- **Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups**: collateral-free loans with guarantee cover (limit raised to ₹20 crore in 2025).
- **Public procurement**: exemption from prior-turnover, prior-experience and EMD conditions on GeM and central tenders.
- State startup policies (Karnataka, Gujarat, Kerala, Telangana, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, UP, Odisha…) add rent, patent and marketing reimbursements, usually requiring DPIIT recognition.

## What it does not do

- It does **not** exempt you from GST, TDS, ROC filings, statutory audit or labour registrations.
- It does **not** by itself give the tax holiday — the IMB application is separate and selective.
- It does **not** stop income-tax scrutiny of valuations entirely (section 56(2)(x) and transfer pricing still apply).
- It is **not** a loan or grant; funds flow through incubators, banks and VCs with their own criteria.

:::tip
Apply the week after incorporation. Recognition is free, carries no obligations, and several state schemes and incubator programmes count “recognised since” dates.
:::

## Keeping the status

Recognition lapses when you cross 10 years or ₹200 crore turnover, or on merger/reconstruction. Update the portal when your details change; file the annual self-certification for labour laws if you use it. A DPIIT number is routinely verified by procurement portals, so keep the certificate PDF with your statutory records.
