Selling online puts you under a layer of consumer law that offline traders rarely meet: the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**, its **E-Commerce Rules, 2020** (substantially amended on 9 September 2026, in force **1 January 2027**), the **Legal Metrology** rules as applied to listings, the **Dark Patterns Guidelines (2023)**, **ASCI** advertising codes, and the **IT Rules** for platforms. Penalties come from the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) and consumer commissions, and marketplaces enforce most of it on sellers contractually.

## Who is who

- **E-commerce entity**: anyone who owns, operates or manages a digital facility for e-commerce — your own store counts.
- **Marketplace entity**: a platform for third-party sellers (Amazon, Flipkart). **Inventory entity**: sells its own stock (your D2C store).
- **Seller on a marketplace**: you, with duties in Rule 6.

## Duties of every e-commerce entity (Rule 4) — including your own store

- Be an Indian-incorporated entity or a foreign company with an Indian establishment; display **legal name, principal address (HQ and branches), website, customer-care email, landline and mobile**, and the **grievance officer’s** details.
- **Grievance officer**: acknowledge complaints within **48 hours**, resolve within **one month**, give the complainant a copy of the recorded complaint; mandatory partnership with the **National Consumer Helpline** convergence (2026 amendment).
- For imported goods: disclose the **importer** and the **full country of origin** per Legal Metrology.
- No **price manipulation** for unreasonable profit, no discrimination between consumers of the same class, no misleading **search manipulation** (2026).
- **Sponsored listings** clearly labelled (2026).
- **Announced price reductions must show the “prior price” = lowest price in the preceding 30 days** (2026) — keep price history.
- **Seller name on the invoice in the same font size as the platform’s** (2026).
- Comply with the **Dark Patterns Guidelines** and do an **annual self-audit**, displaying a certificate (2026): no false urgency, basket sneaking, confirm-shaming, forced action, subscription traps, interface interference, bait-and-switch, drip pricing, disguised ads, nagging, trick wording, SaaS billing tricks or rogue malware.
- Cancellation charges only if you bear equivalent charges on your own cancellations; no fake reviews; record consent explicitly (no pre-ticked boxes).

## Duties of sellers on marketplaces (Rule 6, as amended)

Provide to the platform (to be displayed): legal name and whether registered, geographic address, customer-care number, website/email, **GSTIN or Udyam number**, product details including **country of origin**, **best-before/use-before** dates, return/refund/exchange, **cost of return shipping**, warranty/guarantee, delivery and payment modes; accurate images and descriptions; written contract with the platform; no false advertising; honour the displayed terms. Sellers must also appoint a grievance officer where they operate their own channels.

## Legal Metrology online

The Packaged Commodities Rules require every listing of a pre-packed good to show the **MRP (inclusive of all taxes), net quantity, manufacturer/packer/importer name and address, common name, month and year of manufacture/import, consumer-care details, and country of origin** (2026 amendment strengthens the country-of-origin display and filters). Mismatch between pack and listing is an offence per product; marketplaces delist. **Dual MRP** is illegal; stickers over the MRP are allowed only in specified cases (revised MRP by law/tax change, with both visible).

## Advertising and claims

**ASCI** (self-regulatory but referenced by CCPA): influencer disclosures (#ad, #sponsored), no exaggerated health/efficacy claims, celebrity due diligence; CCPA **Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements (2022)** with penalties up to ₹10 lakh (₹50 lakh repeat) on manufacturers, advertisers **and endorsers**; the 2025–26 proposed advertising guidelines for small businesses are under consultation — watch the feed. Health supplements and foods have FSSAI claim rules; “organic” needs NPOP/PGS certification; “Made in India” claims follow country-of-origin rules.

## IT Rules and platform duties

If you run a **platform** with user-generated content, reviews or third-party sellers, the IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules 2021 apply: publish terms, appoint a grievance officer (24-hour acknowledgement for certain content, 15-day resolution), remove unlawful content on court/government order within 36 hours, and for larger platforms more. **CERT-In** directions require reporting cyber incidents within **6 hours** and keeping logs for 180 days.

## Foreign investment and models

Under the FDI policy, **marketplace** e-commerce allows 100% FDI (automatic) but **inventory-based** e-commerce (the platform owns the stock it sells to consumers) does **not** permit FDI — a constraint for foreign-funded D2C brands, which therefore sell through “marketplace” structures or wholesale arms. Indian-owned companies without foreign investment can run inventory models freely.

## Consumer complaints and e-Daakhil

Consumers file online at **e-Daakhil**; district commissions handle claims up to ₹50 lakh; mediation is available. A clear return policy, delivery proofs and prompt grievance handling defeat most claims. Unfair contract terms (unilateral termination, excessive penalties) are void against consumers.

- [ ] Product master with GSTIN/Udyam, country of origin, best-before, return-shipping cost, warranty
- [ ] Price-history log to compute the 30-day prior price for every discount
- [ ] Sponsored placements labelled on your own site; ranking parameters disclosed if you rank sellers
- [ ] Grievance officer published; 48-hour acknowledgement workflow; NCH convergence
- [ ] Dark-pattern self-audit done and certificate displayed (platforms)
- [ ] Invoices showing seller name at platform-font size (platform setting)
