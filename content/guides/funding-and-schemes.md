Most Indian businesses are funded by the founder, family and a bank — not venture capital. This guide covers the whole ladder: free and subsidised government money, debt you can actually get in year one, and the rules for taking equity when you are ready.

## Government money for new businesses

| Scheme | What | How much | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PM MUDRA Yojana** | Collateral-free loans via banks/NBFCs | Shishu ≤ ₹50k · Kishore ₹50k–5 lakh · Tarun ₹5–10 lakh · **Tarun Plus ₹10–20 lakh** (repeat borrowers) | Non-farm micro units | Any bank; Udyam helps |
| **PMEGP** (KVIC) | Term loan with **15–35% subsidy** | Projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) / ₹20 lakh (services) | New units; subsidy higher for rural/SC/ST/women | kviconline.gov.in → bank |
| **CGTMSE** | Credit guarantee (75–85%) so banks lend without collateral | Loans up to **₹10 crore** | Micro and small enterprises (Udyam) | Through the lending bank |
| **Stand-Up India** | Greenfield enterprise loans | ₹10 lakh – ₹1 crore | SC/ST and women entrepreneurs | standupmitra.in |
| **Startup India Seed Fund** | Grant + convertible | **₹20 lakh grant** (PoC/prototype) · **₹50 lakh** convertible debenture/debt (market entry) | DPIIT startups < 2 years old, via empanelled incubators | seedfund.startupindia.gov.in |
| **Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups (CGSS)** | Guarantee on loans/venture debt | Up to ₹20 crore (raised 2025) | DPIIT startups | Member banks/NBFCs/AIFs |
| **Fund of Funds for Startups** (+ FoF 2.0 approved 2026) | Invests in SEBI AIFs | Indirect | Startups raise from the funded VCs | SIDBI |
| **MSME credit card** (Budget 2025) | ₹5 lakh limit card for micro enterprises | ₹5 lakh | Udyam micro units | Banks |
| **SME Growth Fund** (Budget 2026) | Equity for high-growth MSMEs | ₹10,000 crore fund | MSMEs | Via fund managers (operational details evolving) |
| **State schemes** | Capital/interest subsidies, rent and patent reimbursements | Varies | Usually Udyam + DPIIT | State single windows |

Also: **TReDS** platforms (RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart) discount your invoices on large buyers — Budget 2026 mandated CPSEs to use them; **NSIC** raw-material assistance; **SIDBI** direct loans for startups (SMILE, Fund of Funds); **export credit** (packing credit in INR/foreign currency, pre- and post-shipment) and **ECGC** cover; **NABARD/agri** schemes for food processing; **PLI** schemes for manufacturing sectors.

Tips: Udyam registration and a clean GST filing history are the two things every scheme checks. Apply through the scheme portal **and** walk into the bank branch — PSU banks have MUDRA/CGTMSE targets and will process a well-documented file.

## Bank and NBFC debt in year one

- **Working-capital overdraft / cash credit** against stock and receivables (needs 1–2 years of GST returns usually; CGTMSE-backed for new units).
- **Term loans** for machinery and fit-outs (PMEGP/CGTMSE).
- **Invoice discounting** (TReDS, KredX, Vayana) once you invoice large companies.
- **Fintech NBFC loans** (Lendingkart, Indifi, FlexiLoans, NeoGrowth) on GST and bank data — fast, 16–28% interest.
- **Venture debt** after an equity round.
- Avoid: personal loans and credit cards as working capital beyond a few months; unregistered money lenders.

Keep company borrowings documented: board resolution, loan agreement, **CHG-1** filing for secured loans within 30 days, and remember **DPT-3** reporting.

## Equity — how it works legally

### Instruments
- **Equity shares**: founders and ESOP pool.
- **CCPS** (compulsorily convertible preference shares): the standard VC instrument — liquidation preference, anti-dilution, conversion on a round.
- **CCDs** (compulsorily convertible debentures): used by angels and the Seed Fund; interest optional, converts into equity.
- **iSAFE / SAFE-like notes**: in India these are usually CCPS with deferred pricing (100x Ventures’ iSAFE); a US-style SAFE does not work under the Companies Act.
- **ESOPs**: a scheme approved by shareholders (special resolution), grants with vesting; DPIIT startups get employee tax deferral; promoters cannot receive ESOPs except in eligible startups for 10 years.

### Mechanics
1. Board and shareholders’ resolutions; amend AoA for new rights.
2. **Valuation report** (registered valuer or merchant banker; CA for some cases) — shares cannot be issued below fair value to residents… and to non-residents the price must be *at or above* fair value.
3. Private placement offer (**PAS-4**), money into a separate bank account, allotment within 60 days, **PAS-3** return of allotment within 15 days, share certificates within 2 months, stamp duty on issue.
4. **Foreign investors**: Entity Master on RBI FIRMS, KYC and FIRC from the bank, **FC-GPR within 30 days** of allotment, sector caps and Press Note 3 (approval for land-border countries). Annual **FLA** return by 15 July.
5. **Shareholders’ agreement**: board seats, information rights, reserved matters, drag/tag, founder vesting, exit.

### Tax points
- **Angel tax is gone** for issues from 1 April 2025.
- Investors still face section 56(2)(x) if *they* receive shares below fair value — keep the valuation report.
- Founders’ share sales: capital gains (12.5% long-term above ₹1.25 lakh without indexation for listed; unlisted shares long-term after 24 months at 12.5%).

## Grants, competitions and non-dilutive money

BIRAC (biotech), MeitY TIDE/GENESIS and SAMRIDH (software), DST NIDHI-PRAYAS and Seed Support (deep tech), Atal Innovation Mission, state innovation challenges, corporate accelerators (Google, Microsoft, AWS credits), CSR-funded incubators. Most pay through incubators and release in tranches against milestones.

## Angels and VCs — what to expect

- Angel networks (IAN, Mumbai Angels, LetsVenture, AngelList India) write ₹25 lakh–3 crore cheques, typically via CCPS or CCDs at a ₹5–30 crore valuation.
- Seed and Series A funds need a private limited company, clean cap table (founders’ equity vested, ESOP pool 7–12%), audited financials and MCA filings in order — diligence lists start with the statutory registers.
- Expect 4–8 weeks from term sheet to money; FEMA filings add two weeks when foreign money is involved.

:::warning
Never take money into a company from the public (friends-of-friends, WhatsApp groups) without a proper private placement to a maximum of 200 people a year — it becomes a “deposit” or a deemed public issue with severe penalties.
:::
