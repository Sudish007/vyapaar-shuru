Most Indian businesses are proprietorships, and most online guides overcomplicate them. A proprietorship is simply **you, trading under a name**. There is no central registry, no certificate and no fee. What you need is a set of **proofs** that banks, marketplaces and payment gateways accept, plus the tax registrations your turnover triggers.

## Sole proprietorship

### What makes it real
Any two of these are normally enough to open a current account and onboard anywhere:

1. **Udyam registration** (free, 15 minutes, Aadhaar + PAN) — the single most useful proof.
2. **GST registration** — only if required or useful (see thresholds below).
3. **Shops & Establishment registration** from the state labour department (needed anyway for a shop or office in most states, within 30 days of opening).
4. Trade licence (physical trade premises), FSSAI (food), professional tax enrolment (levying states).

Your **PAN is the business PAN**. You can use a trade name (“Sharma Textiles”) on invoices and the bank account — the account is opened as “Sharma Textiles (Proprietor: Anil Sharma)”.

### Tax
- Income tax on **slab rates**. Under the default (new) regime there is no tax up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income because of the rebate; 30% starts above ₹24 lakh. You may opt for the old regime with deductions.
- **Presumptive taxation** (Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025): businesses up to ₹3 crore turnover (₹2 crore if more than 5% of receipts are cash) declare 6% of digital receipts / 8% of cash receipts as income; specified professionals up to ₹75 lakh (₹50 lakh with cash > 5%) declare 50%. No books, no tax audit, one advance-tax payment by 15 March. Once you opt out after opting in, you are locked out for five years.
- **GST** registration is mandatory above ₹40 lakh (goods only, most states) or ₹20 lakh (services or mixed), lower in special-category states — and *regardless of turnover* if you supply goods inter-state or sell through a marketplace, import, or want to export under LUT.
- **TDS**: proprietors deduct TDS only if they were subject to tax audit in the previous year — except rent above ₹50,000 a month to a resident (2% under 194-IB) and certain contractor/professional payments above ₹50 lakh (194M), which apply to everyone.

### Limits of the structure
Unlimited personal liability; no co-owners; no equity; business dies with the proprietor (successors inherit assets, not the entity); loans depend on your personal credit. Convert to an LLP or company when you hire a team, carry inventory risk or need outside capital.

## Partnership firm

Two to fifty people carrying on business together under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932.

### Set-up
1. **Partnership deed** on stamp paper (duty by state, ₹500–5,000): name, business, capital, profit ratio, remuneration and interest to partners, duties, admission/retirement/death, dissolution, dispute resolution.
2. **Registration with the state Registrar of Firms** (Form 1, fee by state). Optional in law but practically necessary: an unregistered firm cannot sue third parties or its own partners to enforce contractual rights, and banks and marketplaces ask for the certificate. Timelines range from a day (online states) to a month.
3. **PAN** for the firm (Form 49A via Protean), then GST, Udyam, bank account.

### Tax
Flat **30%** + 12% surcharge above ₹1 crore + 4% cess on firm profits; partner remuneration and interest deductible within limits; profit share exempt for partners. **Presumptive taxation is available** to partnership firms (not LLPs). Tax audit above ₹1 crore (₹10 crore digital) applies.

### Liability
Every partner is personally and jointly liable for the firm’s debts, including those created by another partner. That single fact is why most new partner-run businesses now choose an LLP: the extra ₹5,000 and a week of paperwork buy limited liability.

## Converting later

A proprietor cannot “convert” — you incorporate a company/LLP and transfer the business (assets, contracts, GST registration via fresh application and ITC transfer in ITC-02, trademarks by assignment). Capital gains are exempt if the proprietor holds at least 50% of the new company for five years and other conditions in the Act are met. A registered partnership firm can convert into an LLP (Form 17) or a company (URC-1).

:::tip
Open the current account early even as a proprietor. Mixing business and personal money is legal, but it makes the 6%/50% presumptive computation hard to defend if the department asks for bank statements, and it kills any chance of a business loan.
:::
