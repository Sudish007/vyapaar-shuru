GST is one tax on almost every sale of goods or services in India, charged by the seller and credited down the chain. Registration gives you a **GSTIN** and the duty to file returns. The question is not “should I register?” but “am I **required** to, and if not, does registering **help** me?”

## Thresholds — when registration becomes mandatory

| Your supplies | Threshold (aggregate turnover, all-India, per PAN) |
|---|---|
| Only goods, in a “normal” state | **₹40 lakh** |
| Services, or goods + services | **₹20 lakh** |
| Special-category states (Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura, Uttarakhand, Puducherry, Telangana for goods) | **₹20 lakh** goods / **₹10 lakh** services (varies by state notification) |

Aggregate turnover includes exempt and export sales and everything under the same PAN across India, but not GST itself.

## Mandatory regardless of turnover (section 24)

- **Inter-state supply of goods** (services may cross state lines up to ₹20 lakh without registering).
- **Selling through an e-commerce operator** that collects TCS (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho…). Since 1 October 2023 a limited relaxation lets unregistered sellers make *intra-state* supplies through an ECO with an enrolment number and under conditions; in practice marketplaces still require a GSTIN for taxable goods.
- Casual taxable persons (exhibitions in another state), non-resident taxable persons.
- Persons liable to pay **reverse charge** (e.g., importing services from abroad — foreign SaaS subscriptions).
- Agents, input service distributors, OIDAR suppliers, TDS/TCS deductors under GST.

:::law
Importing goods does not itself require GST registration, but you cannot claim the IGST paid at customs as credit without one — so every commercial importer registers. Exporters register to file a LUT and claim refunds.
:::

## Voluntary registration — when it pays

- Your customers are **businesses** that want input credit on your invoice.
- You buy a lot of GST-paid inputs and want the credit (inventory businesses, agencies with software spend).
- You **export** and want zero-rating with refunds instead of absorbing tax.
- A marketplace, payment gateway or bank asks for it as a business proof.

The cost: returns every month or quarter from the day you register, late fees of ₹50 a day (₹20 for nil returns) if you miss, and the obligation to charge GST on every taxable sale.

## The three registration routes (2026)

| Route | Who | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regular (REG-01)** | Everyone | 7 working days after Aadhaar OTP; up to **30 days** with physical verification | Officer may raise a query (REG-03) — answer in 7 days |
| **Simplified — Rule 14A** (from 1 Nov 2025) | Monthly output tax on B2B supplies ≤ ₹2.5 lakh; Aadhaar-authenticated | **3 working days** | Opt in on REG-01; one registration per PAN per state; exit via REG-32 |
| **E-commerce warehouse — Rule 14B** (recommended by the 57th Council on 8 Oct 2026; slated for 1 Nov 2026) | Goods sold *only* through TCS-collecting marketplaces; output tax ≤ ₹2.5 lakh/month; home-state registration exists | Electronic, after ECO consent within 7 working days | Declare the marketplace warehouse as your principal place in that state — no office needed there. Await the notification before filing. |

From **1 November 2026** the Council’s process reforms also bring a unified document list in REG-01 with drop-downs, auto-sanction of excess cash-ledger refunds and 90% provisional refunds for exporters and inverted-duty cases.

## Documents

**Everyone**
- PAN of the business (proprietor’s own PAN) and of promoters/partners/directors
- Aadhaar of promoters and the authorised signatory (OTP or biometric authentication)
- Photographs (JPEG, ≤100 KB)
- **Proof of principal place of business**: electricity bill, property-tax receipt or municipal khata (owned) **plus** rent agreement or consent letter/NOC (rented or shared). Virtual offices work in many states if the provider gives a proper agreement and the premises exist — the officer may visit.
- Bank proof (cancelled cheque, statement or passbook first page) — may be added **within 30 days** of registration or before filing the first GSTR-1/IFF; registration is suspended if missing.

**Companies and LLPs**: certificate of incorporation, board resolution/authorisation letter for the signatory, DSC of the signatory (companies must sign with DSC).
**Partnerships**: deed and registration certificate.

## Procedure

1. gst.gov.in → Services → Registration → New Registration. Part A: PAN, mobile, email → OTPs → **TRN**.
2. Part B within 15 days: business details (trade name, constitution, state, reason “crossing threshold”/“voluntary”/“e-commerce”), promoters, authorised signatory, principal and additional places, goods/services (top five HSN/SAC), bank, state-specific info (professional tax numbers), verification.
3. **Aadhaar authentication**: OTP link to the signatory; if the system flags the application (common in some states — Bihar, Delhi, Karnataka, Punjab, Gujarat and others run biometric pilots), you book a slot at a **GST Suvidha Kendra** for biometric verification and document check.
4. Approval → GSTIN and certificate (REG-06). Display the certificate at the premises and the GSTIN on the name board.
5. Within 30 days: add bank account; enable e-invoicing if already above ₹5 crore; set the invoice series; opt for composition/QRMP if eligible.

## Rejections and how to avoid them

- Electricity bill in a third party’s name without a consent letter — the top rejection reason.
- Rent agreement not registered or expired; agreement mentioning a different floor/unit than the bill.
- Mismatch between the name on PAN and the application (“Pvt. Ltd.” vs “Private Limited”).
- HSN codes that do not fit the stated business.
- Not answering REG-03 queries within seven working days — the application is rejected and you start again.

## After registration

- Charge GST on every taxable invoice with the mandatory fields (see the compliance guide).
- File **GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B** monthly (or quarterly under QRMP) from the registration month — even nil returns.
- Composition dealers file CMP-08 quarterly and GSTR-4 annually.
- Amend the registration (REG-14) within 15 days of any change in address, partners/directors or business.
- Cancel voluntarily (REG-16) if you stop; file the final return (GSTR-10) within three months of cancellation.

:::warning
Beware of registering in several states “to be safe”. Each GSTIN means separate returns and books. You need a GSTIN only in a state where you have a place of business (including stock in a warehouse). Rule 14B is designed exactly to remove the office requirement for small marketplace sellers.
:::
