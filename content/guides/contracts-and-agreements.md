Indian contract law is simple and old (the Contract Act of 1872), e-signatures are valid, and courts enforce clear written terms — slowly. The goal of your early contracts is not litigation; it is to make expectations explicit so that disputes never reach a court, and to make your company **diligence-ready** when money or an acquirer arrives.

## The founders’ layer

### Founders’ agreement (pre-incorporation) → Shareholders’ agreement (post)
Equity split and **vesting** (4 years, 1-year cliff is standard; reverse vesting of founder shares on exit), roles and time commitment, salaries, decision rights and deadlock, **IP assignment** of everything created for the venture (pre- and post-incorporation), confidentiality, non-solicit, what happens on departure (good/bad leaver, buy-back at fair value or nominal), new-founder admission, exit and drag/tag rights. Mirror the key terms into the **Articles of Association** — only AoA terms bind the company and third parties.

### LLP agreement
See the LLP guide: capital, profit shares, remuneration, admission/retirement, IP, disputes. File Form 3 within 30 days.

## People

- **Employment agreement** (see hiring): confidentiality, **IP assignment with moral-rights waiver**, non-solicit (12 months is enforceable), notice, probation, policies incorporated by reference. Post-termination **non-compete is void** under section 27 of the Contract Act — use confidentiality, garden leave and deferred compensation instead.
- **Contractor / consultant agreement**: deliverables, acceptance, fees, TDS and GST handling, IP assignment (without it the contractor owns the copyright), warranties, confidentiality, independent-contractor status.
- **Internship letter**: duration, stipend, learning objectives, confidentiality, IP.
- **ESOP scheme and grant letters**: approved by special resolution (ordinary for private companies in some cases), vesting, exercise price and period, treatment on exit.
- **Advisor agreements**: equity for time (0.25–1%), vesting, confidentiality.

## Customers

- **Terms of service / terms and conditions** for websites and apps (plus **privacy notice** per DPDP and the **refund/shipping** policies required by gateways and consumer law).
- **Master Services Agreement + Statements of Work** for B2B services: scope, change control, acceptance, fees and milestones, payment terms with interest, IP (yours in the platform, theirs in deliverables, or licence), warranties and limitation of liability (cap at fees paid in 12 months is typical), indemnities, confidentiality, data protection (DPDP processor clauses), termination, non-solicit, governing law and disputes.
- **SaaS subscription agreement**: licence grant, SLA and credits, data ownership and portability, security, auto-renewal with notice, suspension for non-payment, export-control and acceptable-use.
- **Quotation and purchase-order terms** for product businesses: price validity, GST extra/inclusive, delivery (Incoterms for exports), risk and title transfer, warranty and returns, retention of title until paid.

## Vendors and operations

- **Manufacturing / supply agreement**: specs, QC and rejection, MOQ, lead times, price changes, tooling, exclusivity, IP, recall and defect liability, payment, audit rights.
- **Distribution / franchise / reseller agreement**: territory, targets, pricing (resale price maintenance is restricted under competition law — suggest, do not fix), marketing funds, brand standards, term and termination, non-compete during the term.
- **Lease / leave-and-licence**: term, lock-in, rent escalation, deposit and its refund timeline, maintenance, permitted use (commercial), registration if over 11 months, GST on rent (18% if the landlord is registered; reverse charge if you are a company renting residential premises).
- **Logistics and 3PL**: liability for loss/damage, COD remittance timelines, RTO handling, insurance.
- **NDA**: mutual, 2–3 years, carve-outs, no obligation to transact; do not over-rely — investors rarely sign them.

## Formalities

- **Stamp duty** is a **state** matter: agreements generally attract nominal duty (₹100–500 in most states for general agreements; higher for leases, share transfers 0.015%, LLP agreements by contribution). Pay via e-stamp paper or state e-stamping portals; an unstamped document is inadmissible until duty and penalty (up to 10×) are paid — fix before a dispute, not during.
- **E-signatures**: Aadhaar eSign and DSC-based signatures are legally valid under the IT Act; click-wrap and typed signatures are enforceable as contracts though not “electronic signatures” in the statutory sense; wills, negotiable instruments, trusts, powers of attorney and real-estate conveyances must be wet-signed. Use DigiLocker/eSign providers (Leegality, SignDesk, Digio) for audit trails.
- **Registration** at the sub-registrar is mandatory for leases over 11 months, sale of immovable property and some gifts; optional for most other agreements.
- **Board approval**: companies should approve significant contracts and all related-party transactions in board minutes; keep an agreements register.

## Dispute clauses that work

- **Arbitration** (Arbitration and Conciliation Act): sole arbitrator, seat in your city, institutional rules (MCIA, DIAC, SIAC for international) or ad hoc; faster than courts, enforceable like a decree. For small-value B2B disputes, **MSME Samadhaan** (if you are an MSE supplier) and **commercial courts** (claims above ₹3 lakh, with pre-institution mediation) are alternatives.
- **Governing law** India; **exclusive jurisdiction** of courts at your location; **mediation first** clauses are now expected (Mediation Act 2023).
- **Interest** on late payments stated explicitly (MSMED default is 3× bank rate for MSE suppliers).
- **Limitation**: three years for most contract claims — diarise.

## A minimum contract kit for month one

1. Founders’/shareholders’ agreement + aligned AoA
2. Employment agreement and offer-letter template; contractor agreement; NDA
3. Customer terms (ToS + privacy + refunds) or MSA/SOW template
4. Vendor PO terms; manufacturing agreement if you make products
5. Lease/licence for premises, registered if needed
6. ESOP scheme (when hiring key people)

Templates from reputable Indian sources (incubators, law-firm startup kits, Startup India’s templates) are a fine start; spend lawyer money on the shareholders’ agreement and the customer MSA — those carry the risk.
