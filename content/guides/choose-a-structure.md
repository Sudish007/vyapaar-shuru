The structure you pick on day one decides four things for years: **who is liable** when something goes wrong, **how much tax** you pay and when, **how much paperwork** you carry every year, and **whether anyone can invest** in you. Everything else — GST, licences, hiring — layers on top and is mostly the same whichever you choose.

## The five options at a glance

| | Sole proprietorship | Partnership firm | LLP | One Person Company | Private limited |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legal person? | No — it is you | No (registered firm can sue) | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Liability | Unlimited, personal | Unlimited, joint | Limited to contribution | Limited to shares | Limited to shares |
| Minimum people | 1 | 2 | 2 partners | 1 member + 1 nominee | 2 shareholders, 2 directors |
| Set-up cost (govt + typical professional) | ₹0–2,000 | ₹2,000–10,000 | ₹6,000–15,000 | ₹7,000–20,000 | ₹8,000–25,000 |
| Time to start | Same day | 1–4 weeks | 10–15 working days | 7–15 working days | 7–15 working days |
| Income tax | Your slab rates (nil to ₹12 lakh under the default regime) | 30% + surcharge + cess | 30% + surcharge + cess | 25%/22% + surcharge + cess | 25%/22% + surcharge + cess |
| Presumptive tax available? | Yes (6%/8% or 50%) | Yes (not LLPs) | No | No | No |
| Audit | Only tax audit above limits | Only tax audit | Above ₹40 lakh turnover or ₹25 lakh contribution | Every year | Every year |
| Annual filings | ITR | ITR | Form 11, Form 8, ITR | AOC-4, MGT-7A, ITR, DIR-3 KYC | AOC-4, MGT-7A, ADT-1, DPT-3, ITR, DIR-3 KYC |
| Can raise equity? | No | No | Partners only | No outside shareholders | Yes — angels, VCs, ESOPs |
| DPIIT startup recognition | No | Registered firm: yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Tax holiday (old 80-IAC) | No | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Foreign investors | Not practical | Approval route | Automatic route (most sectors) | Not allowed as member | Automatic route |

:::law
Income tax rates quoted are the regimes in force after the Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect on 1 April 2026; Budget 2026 changed no rates. The concessional company rate is 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = **25.17%** all-in for a company that forgoes exemptions; the default is 25% (turnover up to ₹400 crore) or 30%, plus surcharge of 7%/12% above ₹1/₹10 crore and 4% cess. LLPs and firms pay 30% + 12% surcharge above ₹1 crore + 4% cess.
:::

## A decision rule that fits most founders

1. **Solo, selling your own skill or small volumes, not raising money** → sole proprietorship. You can always incorporate later and transfer the business (GST registration moves with a fresh application; trademarks are assigned).
2. **Two or more people, a services or trading business, no plans to sell equity** → LLP. Limited liability, no mandatory audit until ₹40 lakh turnover, no board meetings or AGMs, profit shares flow to partners tax-free after the LLP’s 30%.
3. **Building a product or brand, hiring, planning to raise angel or VC money, or foreign customers who want a “company”** → private limited. It is the only structure investors, ESOP plans and acquirers work with natively.
4. **Solo founder who needs a company (client insists, liability-heavy work) but has no co-founder** → OPC. Same compliance as a private company minus the AGM; converts to a private limited whenever a second shareholder arrives.
5. **Mission-first, grant-funded** → Section 8 company (see the non-profit path).

## What each choice really costs per year

Running costs matter more than set-up costs.

- **Proprietorship:** a CA for ITR-3/ITR-4 (₹1,500–6,000) and GST filing if registered (₹500–2,000 a month). Total ₹5,000–30,000.
- **LLP:** Form 11 + Form 8 + ITR-5 + GST: ₹12,000–35,000. Audit only once you cross the limits.
- **Private limited / OPC:** statutory audit (₹10,000–50,000), ROC filings by a CS/CA (₹8,000–20,000), GST, TDS and payroll compliance. Realistically **₹40,000–1 lakh a year** for a small company — the price of being investable. Small companies (paid-up capital up to ₹10 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore since 1 December 2025) get lighter rules: two board meetings a year, MGT-7A, no cash-flow statement, no CARO.

## Tax arithmetic that surprises people

- **LLP vs company at low profits.** On ₹20 lakh profit an LLP pays about ₹6.24 lakh (31.2%); a company pays ₹5.03 lakh (25.17%) but founders pay slab tax again on dividends. If you will reinvest profits, the company wins; if you will take everything home, the LLP (or a proprietorship under slab rates) usually wins.
- **Proprietor under presumptive taxation.** A consultant billing ₹60 lakh declares ₹30 lakh income (50%) and pays slab tax on that — around ₹5.6 lakh under the default regime — with no books. The same income in a company costs more once salary and dividends are taxed.
- **Partner remuneration** in an LLP is deductible within limits (₹3 lakh or 90% of the first ₹6 lakh of book profit, then 60%), which lowers the effective rate for working partners.

## When to convert

Convert proprietorship → company when you (a) sign a term sheet, (b) cross roughly ₹2–3 crore of turnover with employees, or (c) take on liability you cannot personally carry (imports, manufacturing, platforms with user data). LLP → company conversion is a formal MCA process (Form URC-1) and takes 1–2 months; plan it before fundraising, not during.

:::tip
Whatever you choose, register on **Udyam** (free) and file the **trademark** early. Both are independent of structure and both get harder to fix later.
:::

## Checklist before you decide

- Who is liable if a customer sues, a shipment is seized, or a loan defaults?
- Will you raise equity in the next three years?
- Do you need the presumptive scheme (only proprietors and partnership firms get it)?
- Do foreign customers or investors need a company?
- Can you afford ₹40,000–1 lakh a year of company compliance before revenue?
