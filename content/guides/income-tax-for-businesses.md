On **1 April 2026** the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. The rules you will meet as a founder are substantively the same — rates, thresholds, presumptive schemes, TDS — but almost every **section number changed**, the “previous year/assessment year” pair became a single **tax year**, and forms were re-issued under the Income-tax Rules, 2026. Expect older articles, CAs and even portals to quote both numbers for a while.

## Who pays what

| Entity | Rate on profits | Surcharge | Cess | Effective (typical small business) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proprietor (individual) | Slab rates (default regime: nil to ₹4 lakh, 5–30% in steps; rebate makes income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free) | 10–25% above ₹50 lakh | 4% | 0% to ~31% |
| Partnership firm / LLP | **30%** | 12% above ₹1 crore | 4% | 31.2% |
| Domestic company — default | 25% (turnover ≤ ₹400 crore in the reference year) or 30% | 7% > ₹1 cr, 12% > ₹10 cr | 4% | 26% |
| Domestic company — concessional (old 115BAA) | **22%**, no exemptions/holidays | 10% flat | 4% | **25.17%** |
| New manufacturing company (old 115BAB) | 15% | 10% | 4% | 17.16% — only for companies that began production by 31 Mar 2024 unless extended |

Companies also pay tax on **dividends in shareholders’ hands** (slab rates, TDS 10% above ₹10,000) and **buy-back** proceeds are taxed as dividends. There is no tax-free way to take money out of a company; salary (deductible to the company) plus dividend is the usual mix.

## Presumptive taxation (section 58)

For proprietors and partnership firms (not LLPs or companies):

- **Business**: declare 8% of turnover (6% of receipts received digitally) as income, turnover up to **₹2 crore**, or **₹3 crore** if cash receipts are at most 5% of total.
- **Specified professions** (legal, medical, engineering, architecture, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, IT, film artists, company secretaries): declare **50%** of gross receipts, up to **₹50 lakh**, or **₹75 lakh** with cash ≤ 5%.
- Goods carriages: fixed per vehicle per month.

No books of account, no tax audit, advance tax in one shot by 15 March, return in **ITR-4**. Under the 2025 Act the scheme is explicit that **no other deductions or losses** can be set against presumptive income. If you opt out after opting in (business scheme), you cannot opt back for five years and must keep books and get audited if above ₹1 crore. Many freelancers actually pay *less* under presumptive than by claiming real expenses — do the comparison once a year.

## Books, audit and returns

- **Books** are compulsory (section 62) when business income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh or turnover ₹25 lakh in any of the three preceding years (₹1.2 lakh/₹10 lakh for professionals), and for every company and LLP anyway.
- **Tax audit** (section 63): turnover above **₹1 crore**, or **₹10 crore** when cash receipts *and* cash payments are each ≤ 5%; professionals above **₹50 lakh**. Report in **Form 26** (old 3CA/3CB-3CD) by **30 September**; return by **31 October**.
- **Return forms and dates**: ITR-3 (proprietor with books) / ITR-4 (presumptive) by 31 July; ITR-5 (firms, LLPs) 31 July or 31 October if audited; ITR-6 (companies) 31 October; 30 November with transfer pricing. Budget 2026 proposed extending the window for belated/revised returns to **31 March** on payment of a fee — check the final Act.
- **Advance tax**: four instalments on 15 June (15%), 15 September (45%), 15 December (75%), 15 March (100%) once liability exceeds ₹10,000; interest 1% a month on shortfalls.
- Keep books and vouchers **six years** from the end of the tax year (eight for companies under the Companies Act).

## Deductions and traps that matter in year one

- **Pay MSME suppliers within 45 days** (15 if no agreement) or the expense is disallowed until paid — continued from the old section 43B(h).
- **Cash**: no expense above ₹10,000 paid in cash per day per person is deductible; receiving ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day or per transaction attracts a penalty equal to the amount (section 269ST regime).
- **TDS not deducted** on rent, contractors, professionals → 30% of the expense disallowed until the TDS is paid.
- **Depreciation** on laptops/software 40%, furniture 10%, plant 15%, with additional depreciation for manufacturers.
- **Start-up expenses** (incorporation, market research before commencement) are amortised over five years, not expensed at once.
- Founders’ **ESOPs** in a DPIIT-recognised startup enjoy TDS deferral on exercise (up to 48 months, or sale/exit, whichever is earlier).

## Startup tax holiday (old section 80-IAC)

A DPIIT-recognised **private limited company or LLP**, incorporated after 1 April 2016 and (per Budget 2025) before **1 April 2030**, with turnover under ₹100 crore in the claim year, can apply to the Inter-Ministerial Board for a **100% deduction of profits for any three consecutive years in its first ten**. Approval is selective (innovation, scalability, job creation); the holiday does not shelter you from minimum alternate tax if you stay on the default regime, and companies on the 22% regime cannot combine it with that rate. Angel tax on share premium was **abolished** for shares issued from 1 April 2025.

## Tax on money coming in and going out

- **TDS suffered**: clients deduct 10%/2% (professional/technical fees), 1%/2% (contracts), 0.1% (marketplaces). Everything lands in your **AIS/Form 26AS**; claim it in the return and reconcile — mismatches trigger notices.
- **Foreign income**: an Indian resident entity is taxed on worldwide income; foreign withholding is credited under the treaty (file Form 67 before the return).
- **GST is not income** — book it separately; the 2025 Act treats turnover exclusive of GST for thresholds when GST is separately shown.

:::tip
Budget 2026 did not change rates but rationalised TDS/TCS and promised simpler ITR forms under the new Act. Treat 2026-27 as a transition year: keep the old-section mapping handy (44AD → 58, 44AB → 63, 115BAC → 202, 80-IAC → re-numbered) when reading notices or CA notes.
:::
