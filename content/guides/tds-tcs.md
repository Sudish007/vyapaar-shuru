Tax Deducted at Source makes **you** the tax collector for the government on payments you make. Companies, LLPs and firms must deduct from day one; proprietors only once they were liable to tax audit in the previous year (with two exceptions below). The sections were renumbered in the Income-tax Act, 2025; the old numbers are still how everyone talks, so both are given.

## Step zero: get a TAN

A Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (Form 49B, ₹77, via Protean or bundled in SPICe+/FiLLiP). Quote it on every challan, return and certificate. Deducting without a TAN is itself an offence.

## The payments a small business meets

| Payment | Old section | Threshold (per year unless stated) | Rate (resident payee with PAN) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salary | 192 | Taxable salary above the basic exemption | Slab rates, averaged monthly |
| Rent of land/building/furniture | 194-I | ₹50,000 **per month** (raised from ₹2.4 lakh a year in 2025) | 10% (2% for plant/machinery) |
| Rent paid by an individual/HUF not under audit | 194-IB | ₹50,000 per month | 2% |
| Contractors, job work, freight, ad agencies | 194C | ₹30,000 per bill or ₹1 lakh per year | 1% individual/HUF payee, 2% others |
| Professional / technical fees, royalty, director fees | 194J | ₹50,000 | 10% (2% for technical services and call centres) |
| Commission / brokerage (incl. influencers on commission) | 194H | ₹20,000 | 2% |
| Interest other than on securities | 194A | ₹10,000 (bank), ₹5,000 others | 10% |
| Purchase of goods from one seller | 194Q | ₹50 lakh in a year, buyer turnover > ₹10 crore | 0.1% on the excess |
| E-commerce operator paying sellers | 194-O | Nil (₹5 lakh for individual/HUF sellers who furnish PAN) | 0.1% of gross sales (5% without PAN) |
| Payments to non-residents (services, software, royalties) | 195 | Nil | Treaty or Act rate; file 15CA/15CB |
| Dividends paid by the company | 194 | ₹10,000 per shareholder | 10% |
| Cash withdrawals above ₹1 crore (₹20 lakh if returns not filed) | 194N | — | 2–5% deducted by the bank |

No PAN from the payee → deduct at **20%** (or the higher rate). Lower/nil deduction certificates (Form 13) exist for payees with losses.

## Deadlines

- **Deposit** TDS by the **7th** of the following month (**30 April** for March). Government-style challan 281 on the e-filing portal; late deposit costs 1.5% a month interest and, if very late, prosecution exposure.
- **Quarterly returns** on TRACES: **24Q** (salary), **26Q** (other residents), **27Q** (non-residents) by 31 July, 31 October, 31 January, **31 May**. Late fee ₹200 a day up to the TDS amount.
- **Certificates**: Form 16 (salary) by 15 June; Form 16A within 15 days of the return due date each quarter.
- **Disallowance**: if you did not deduct (or deducted and did not deposit by the return filing date), **30%** of the expense is disallowed in that year.

## TCS — when you collect tax

- **GST TCS** (separate law): marketplaces collect 0.5% of your net sales and you claim it in your electronic cash ledger — see the marketplaces guide.
- **Income-tax TCS** under section 206C: sellers of scrap, timber, minerals and motor vehicles above ₹10 lakh collect 1%; **foreign remittances under LRS** are collected by banks (20% above ₹10 lakh for most purposes, 5% for education/medical, nil on education loans) — the 2025 Budget raised the threshold from ₹7 lakh to ₹10 lakh; business import payments through proper channels are not LRS and attract no TCS. The old 206C(1H) TCS on sale of goods above ₹50 lakh was **removed** from 1 April 2025.
- Return **27EQ** quarterly; certificate 27D.

## Paying non-residents

Software subscriptions, contractors abroad, foreign ads: decide whether the payment is taxable in India (royalty/fees for technical services vs business income with no permanent establishment), check the treaty (US, UK, Singapore and most others cap FTS/royalty at 10–15%, and business profits are not taxable without a PE), obtain the payee’s **Tax Residency Certificate and Form 10F**, and file **Form 15CA** (and **15CB** from a CA above ₹5 lakh per year) through the bank. Most SaaS payments to the US are not “royalty” after the 2021 Supreme Court ruling, but banks still want the paperwork. **Equalisation levy** on online advertising was abolished from 1 April 2025.

## Practical system for a small company

1. Tag every vendor in your accounting software with their TDS section when you onboard them; collect PAN and (for individuals) whether they are under audit.
2. Let the software compute TDS on each bill; review the monthly TDS register on the 3rd; pay by the 7th.
3. Quarterly, run the return through TRACES-compatible software (ClearTDS, Saral, Winman or your CA).
4. Reconcile Form 26AS of your payees only if they complain — reconcile **your own** 26AS/AIS quarterly for TDS suffered.
5. Keep Form 16A/16 issuance in the compliance calendar; employees will ask in June.

:::warning
Two traps for proprietors who think TDS is “for companies”: rent above ₹50,000 a month to a resident individual (deduct 2% under 194-IB once a year) and payments to contractors or professionals above ₹50 lakh a year (5% under 194M). Both apply even without a TAN — use your PAN in Form 26QC/26QD.
:::
